The businesses that win will be the ones with AI-capable people

The next decade belongs to the companies whose people can direct AI. Not a smaller payroll. More finished work per person, with the Western Canada numbers and the real funding.

By Matt Haynes, founder of KR8TIV AI · November 27, 2026 · 17 min read

The short answer

  • Output per person is the score that matters. Statistics Canada measures labour productivity as real GDP per hour worked. It rose 1.0 percent in the second quarter of 2026, the largest rise since 2020. One rebound does not staff a company. Trained people do.

Why does output per employee matter more than headcount?

Canada's scoreboard is labour productivity: real GDP per hour worked in the business sector. In the second quarter of 2026 it rose 1.0 percent, the largest quarterly increase since the second quarter of 2020. Hours worked edged down 0.1 percent. Unit labour costs still rose 0.6 percent, a fifth straight quarter. (Statistics Canada, The Daily, 3 September 2026)

When Erik Brynjolfsson, Danielle Li and Lindsey Raymond gave a generative assistant to 5,172 customer-support agents, issues resolved per hour rose 15 percent on average. Less experienced and lower-skilled workers improved speed and quality. The most experienced saw small speed gains and small quality declines. Quarterly Journal of Economics, May 2025. (Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond)

The biggest documented gains did not come from removing the newest person. They came from giving that person the habits of the best person, with a human still on the call.

The Edmonton version is in what AI can and cannot do. The tool spread know-how. It did not delete the job.

BCG's 2025 survey covered more than 10,600 people in 11 countries. More than three-quarters of leaders and managers use generative AI several times a week. Front-line regular use had stalled at 51 percent, and it is sharply higher after at least five hours of training plus in-person training and coaching. Only one-third said they had been properly trained. (BCG, 2025) Five hours is a floor.

How does the Canada-Alberta Productivity Grant treat AI training?

On 8 October 2026 every trainee became eligible for 75 percent, employed family members could be trainees, and owners could be trainees. (alberta.ca, guidelines)

The employer cap is $100,000 per fiscal year, 1 April to 31 March. The person cap is 75 percent, up to $5,000. More than one application is allowed. The caps still bind. You pay 100 percent first. The ministry of Jobs, Economy, Trade and Immigration decides, only through the portal, not by phone or email.

The guidelines' own example, not our price: $3,000 of eligible costs pays $2,250 from the grant and $750 from the employer. At $12,000 the grant stops at $5,000, and the employer pays $7,000.

A $2,000 seminar, per trainee

KR8TIV's seminar is $2,000 CAD for the group, not per person. Checked on the live training page on 27 November 2026. Say eight named trainees, and the ministry accepts an even split.

  • Eligible cost: $2,000 divided by 8, which is $250.
  • Grant at 75 percent: $187.50 per trainee.
  • Employer share: $62.50 per trainee.
  • If all eight are approved: $1,500 back.
  • You still write a cheque for $2,000 first.

The $5,000 cap is idle at $187.50. At $1,500 back per person, 66 people use $99,000 of the employer cap. At $5,000 each, 20 people use the whole cap. If the ministry treats the full $2,000 as one person's course, the grant is $1,500 and the employer pays $500. File the invoice you can defend. This is arithmetic, not an approval.

Training must be instructor-led, tied to the job, and done within 52 weeks of receipt. Online counts as Alberta. The provider is a third party whose main work has been training for two years, with a qualified instructor. Not you, not your staff, not a vendor teaching only its own product. Wages, licences, subscriptions, conferences, consulting, coaching and room rental are out. Trainees live in Alberta and are citizens, permanent residents or protected persons: employees, people already offered a job, employed family, or active owners. Contractors, volunteers, board members and unemployed family are out, unless the person is an owner.

Apply at capg.alberta.ca at least 30 days before the start. Training that begins before the status reads Application Received is ineligible. That status flips when the trainee clicks "I confirm," not when you click submit. First come, first served. Start after receipt and before approval, and a decline means the bill is yours. Canada-Alberta Productivity Grant AI training appears as example courses: AI for Business, Generative AI Applications, Microsoft Copilot. A topic is not a purchase order. Checked 27 November 2026.

What federal money exists, and who is it actually for?

PrairiesCan is repayable

In Alberta, Saskatchewan and Manitoba, a business can get an interest-free repayable contribution of $250,000 to $5 million, up to 50 percent. A national $500 million add took the initiative to $700 million. Not-for-profits that support firms can be non-repayable up to 90 percent. One approval a calendar year. Projects end 31 March 2031. You need a year of operations, one full-time employee and a prairie facility, adopting AI or commercializing above technology readiness level 6, with the other money confirmed. Research at levels 1 to 5 and ongoing operating costs are out. Stacked government money stops at 50 percent of capital costs and 75 percent of other costs. Costs can reach back 12 months before a signed application is received, and not before 16 April 2024. A seminar does not clear the floor. Phone 1-888-338-9378. (PrairiesCan)

The home page (26 October 2026) says intake to 31 December 2030. The apply page (16 October 2026) says 31 December 2029. The program information page (3 November 2026) scores advanced manufacturing, clean resources, clean technology, digital industries, health and biosciences, value-added processing, value-added agriculture and inclusiveness. Write to that list, and confirm the end date. Checked 27 November 2026.

## Why does output per employee matter more than headcount?

Canada's scoreboard is labour productivity: real GDP per hour worked in the business sector. In the second quarter of 2026 it rose 1.0 percent, the largest increase since the second quarter of 2020. Hours worked edged down 0.1 percent. Unit labour costs still rose 0.6 percent, a fifth straight quarterly increase. ([Statistics Canada, The Daily, 3 September 2026](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260903/dq260903c-eng.htm))

When Erik Brynjolfsson, Danielle Li and Lindsey Raymond gave a generative assistant to 5,172 customer-support agents, issues resolved per hour rose 15 percent on average. Less experienced and lower-skilled workers improved speed and quality. The most experienced and highest-skilled workers saw small gains in speed and small declines in quality. That is the published abstract in the Quarterly Journal of Economics, May 2025, volume 140, issue 2. ([Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/140/2/889/7990658)) The Edmonton telling of the same study is in [what AI can and cannot do](https://kr8tiv.ai/what-ai-can-and-cant-do-for-your-business/).

> The biggest documented gains did not come from removing the newest person. They came from giving that person the habits of the best person, with a human still on the call.

BCG's 2025 AI at Work survey covered more than 10,600 leaders, managers and frontline white-collar employees in 11 countries and regions. More than three-quarters of leaders and managers say they use generative AI several times a week. Regular use among frontline employees has stalled at 51 percent. Regular use is sharply higher for employees who receive at least five hours of training and have access to in-person training and coaching. Only one-third say they have been properly trained. ([BCG, 2025](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/ai-at-work-momentum-builds-but-gaps-remain))

## How does the Canada-Alberta Productivity Grant treat AI training?

On 8 October 2026 the province posted three changes: every trainee became eligible for 75 percent of eligible training costs, employed family members could be trainees, and business owners could be trainees. The funding box on that same page still says per employed trainee, and it still says the employer contributes 25 percent. Follow the 8 October notice and the October 2026 guidelines: 75 percent for every trainee, until the $5,000 cap. ([alberta.ca](https://www.alberta.ca/canada-alberta-productivity-grant), [October 2026 guidelines](https://open.alberta.ca/dataset/14776419-ee5f-49d3-bfb6-9177d0214ef5/resource/cb980216-d2d1-4ea4-b4c2-df6f71c610a4/download/jeti-canada-alberta-productivity-grant-applicant-guidelines-2026.pdf))

Those caps run per fiscal year, 1 April to 31 March. You can file more than once. The caps still bind. You pay the whole bill, finish, file the completion form, and then the grant pays the eligible share. The ministry of Jobs, Economy, Trade and Immigration will not tell you that you are eligible over the phone or by email. It decides after an application is in the portal.

The guidelines print their own example, not our price. On $3,000 of eligible costs the grant pays $2,250 and the employer pays $750. On $12,000 the grant stops at $5,000 and the employer pays $7,000. The ministry has to call the fee reasonable. It can ask for other quotes.

### A $2,000 seminar, split across a crew

KR8TIV's seminar is $2,000 CAD for the group, not per person, checked on the live [AI training](https://kr8tiv.ai/services/ai-training/) page on 27 November 2026. Say eight named trainees, and the ministry accepts an even split.

- Eligible cost per trainee: $2,000 divided by 8, which is $250.
- Grant at 75 percent: $187.50.
- Employer share: $62.50.
- If all eight are approved: $1,500 back, after the completion form.
- You still write the $2,000 cheque first.

The $5,000 trainee cap does not wake up at $187.50. If the ministry treats the full $2,000 as one person's course, the grant is $1,500 and the employer pays $500. File the invoice you can defend. This is arithmetic, not an approval.

The traps that actually bounce applications:

- The employer has been operational in Alberta for at least one year.
- Training is instructor-led, tied to the person's job, and finished within 52 weeks of the date the application is received.
- Online training counts as Alberta. In-person training outside Alberta needs a written reason, including the Alberta courses you considered.
- The provider is a third party whose main work has been training for at least two years, with a qualified instructor. Not the employer, not a relative, not your own staff, and not a vendor whose course is only how to use that vendor's product.
- Wages, business software licences, subscriptions, conferences, consulting, coaching, mentorship and facility rental are out. A diploma or a degree whose point is the credential is out.
- Trainees live in Alberta and are citizens, permanent residents or persons protected under the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act. Lloydminster, Saskatchewan is the written exception, if the employer operates and is located in Alberta and the person works in Alberta.
- Eligible people: employees, people who already have a job offer, employed family, and owners with an active role. Employed family have to be paid employees, and you have to say why. A conflict of interest can sink the file. Out: board members, unemployed family, temporary foreign workers, and contractors or volunteers who are not owners. A private career college has to be on the registry.

Apply at [capg.alberta.ca](https://capg.alberta.ca/). Alberta recommends you submit at least 30 days before the start. That 30 days is a recommendation. The rule is stricter and different: training that starts before the status reads Application Received is ineligible. The status flips when the trainee clicks "I confirm" in the consent email, not when you click submit. You may pay before approval. You may not start before that status. If you start after the status and before approval, and the file is declined or only partly funded, the bill is yours. First come, first served.

The public page lists example courses under digital and technological skills, including AI for Business, Generative AI Applications and Microsoft Copilot.

## What can British Columbia, Saskatchewan and Manitoba employers apply for?

### British Columbia

WorkBC's Employer Training Grant is open for 2026/27 for training that begins on or after 1 April 2026. The update note is dated 23 March 2026. The dollar caps are in the table above. ([WorkBC](https://www.workbc.ca/employers-industry/funding-programs/bc-employer-training-grant), [what is new](https://www.workbc.ca/employers-industry/funding-programs/bc-employer-training-grant/whats-new))

You apply with a Business BCeID. A third party cannot apply for you, and the participant files their own information form. Citizens, permanent residents and protected persons qualify. Temporary foreign workers and international students do not. Training cannot run past 52 weeks. Diplomas and degrees are out, in full or in part. The result has to be a job, a better job, or more security.

Six factors sort the queue: first-time applicants, small businesses, regions with the greatest shortages, a B.C. public post-secondary trainer, trades apprenticeship at levels 3 or 4, and shortage industries, including AI and quantum computing. Depending on funding, fewer factors means the file can come back unassessed. You are notified within 60 days. You may start before that notice. A denial leaves the invoice with you. After training has started, the reimbursement claim is due within 30 days.

### Manitoba

Building Up Manitoba launched 25 May 2026 with $4 million through the Canada-Manitoba Labour Market Transfer Agreements. Employers need fewer than 500 employees, including affiliates, a full year of operations, and a permanent base in Manitoba. The dollar caps are in the table. ([Program page](https://www.gov.mb.ca/jec/busdev/financial/bump/), [news release](https://news.gov.mb.ca/news/index.html?item=73957))

The province cost-shares half. The money is for a job, a better job, or more security. Family members qualify only after more than three months on the payroll before you apply. The trainer has to be at arm's length, and training has to be the main business. Travel is not reimbursed. Diplomas, degrees, and job-unrelated professional development are out. The page names consulting, coaching, mentorships, subscriptions, conferences and seminars in that last group. A seminar counts only when it is tied to the job result. Training on or after 1 April 2026 can be funded, and the agreement cannot run past the fiscal year you apply in, 1 April to 31 March. The date of the training decides, not the date you pay.

([News release](https://www.saskatchewan.ca/government/news-and-media/2025/march/19/delivering-essential-skills-training-despite-federal-government-cuts)) No replacement employer form turned up in the pages read for this note.

On 18 November 2026 Innovation Saskatchewan put $65,000 into two providers. Co.Labs received $40,000 for AI adoption training for small and medium-sized businesses, and had trained eight businesses since a September 2026 launch. ComIT received $25,000 for unemployed and underemployed people: more than 120 registrations, 24 selected, 21 finished, and 11 employed, four of those in tech. ([Innovation Saskatchewan](https://www.saskatchewan.ca/government/news-and-media/2026/november/18/innovation-saskatchewan-invests-in-ai-skills-training-and-business-adoption)) Provider funding and a people program. Not a cheque an employer files.

## What federal money exists, and who is it for?

### PrairiesCan is repayable, and it starts at $250,000

A national $500 million add, over five years, took the initiative to $700 million. A business gets it interest-free, up to 50 percent, over at most three years, and pays it back. Not-for-profits that support businesses can be non-repayable up to 90 percent. A not-for-profit on a commercial project is normally repayable. One approval a calendar year. Projects end 31 March 2031. Phone 1-888-338-9378. ([PrairiesCan](https://www.canada.ca/en/prairies-economic-development/services/funding/regional-artificial-intelligence-initiative.html), [program information](https://www.canada.ca/en/prairies-economic-development/services/funding/regional-artificial-intelligence-initiative/information.html))

An incorporated for-profit firm needs a year of operations, at least one full-time employee and a staffed facility in Alberta, Saskatchewan or Manitoba. Firms under 500 full-time employees may be preferred. The project adopts AI, or commercializes it above technology readiness level 6, and Canadian AI products may be preferred. Other funding has to be confirmed first. Projects that bring other funding are preferred. Out: research at levels 1 to 5, land, refinancing, lobbying, dues, and ongoing operating costs. For a business or a commercial project, stacked government money stops at 50 percent of eligible capital costs and 75 percent of other eligible costs. Non-commercial projects, and projects by Indigenous recipients, can stack up to 100 percent. Costs can reach back 12 months before a signed application is received, and not before 16 April 2024. A $2,000 seminar does not reach the floor. ([Who can apply](https://www.canada.ca/en/prairies-economic-development/services/funding/regional-artificial-intelligence-initiative/eligibility.html))

Two intake dates are printed. The home page, dated 26 October 2026, says 31 December 2030, or until the money is committed. The apply page, dated 16 October 2026, says 31 December 2029. ([Apply](https://www.canada.ca/en/prairies-economic-development/services/funding/regional-artificial-intelligence-initiative/application-process.html)) Write to the priorities on the program information page, which are not the national aims: advanced manufacturing, clean resources, clean technology, digital industries, health and biosciences, value-added processing of natural resources, value-added agriculture, and inclusiveness. Confirm the date when you file.

### The B.C. federal window has closed

Adoption needs three years of operations in British Columbia and 5 to 500 full-time employees. Commercialization needs one year and 1 to 500. On a page dated 23 November 2026, the status line says applications are not being accepted. The body still says a 2026/27 business intake runs until noon Pacific on 23 November 2026. Today is 27 November, so that noon has passed. Later intakes are described as investments starting after 1 April 2027, for non-profits and smaller firms, with no calendar date. ([PacifiCan](https://www.canada.ca/en/pacific-economic-development/services/funding/regional-artificial-intelligence-initiative.html))

### BDC LIFT is a loan

BDC launched LIFT on 24 April 2026: $500 million, to help over 1,000 small and medium-sized firms. ([News release](https://www.bdc.ca/en/about/mediaroom/news-releases/bdc-launches-lift-getting-canadian-smes-off-the-ai-sidelines)) Loan terms, read the same day on the program page: $25,000 to $2 million, capital payments postponed up to 12 months, amortization up to 6 years including that postponement. You need to be Canadian-based, with at least $1 million in revenue, a current profit, a solid credit history, an up-to-date roadmap, and a qualified Canadian supplier. Training and integration, meaning adoption costs, can be financed. You pay it back.

### IRAP is for firms building the technology

NRC IRAP's AI Assist page, last modified 22 October 2024, is for small and medium-sized firms developing or adapting generative AI and deep learning inside their own products: research, development, testing and validation. Phone 1-877-994-4727. No typical award is published. The page does not describe a reimbursement for a staff course. ([NRC IRAP](https://nrc.canada.ca/en/support-technology-innovation/nrc-irap-support-smes-innovating-artificial-intelligence))

### Amii is a project, not a rebate

On 27 October 2026 PrairiesCan added $2.5 million, for $5.6 million in federal funding, so Amii could partner with 25 new prairie firms on Advanced Technology Projects. Early work was in energy, agriculture and manufacturing. Limited capacity remained when the page was read on 27 November 2026. This is not a course rebate you file. ([Amii](https://www.amii.ca/updates-insights/amii-and-prairiescan-expand-successful-ai-adoption-program))

### CASBE: the researcher applies

A post-secondary researcher applies, with an Alberta firm that has at least two full-time employees and at least two years of operations in the province. Projects sit at technology readiness levels 2 to 6 and finish within two years. Meeting every test does not guarantee funding. The firm does not apply alone.

Read on 27 November 2026, the dates on the same page do not agree. The expression of interest is 18 December 2026 at 4:00 p.m. Mountain Time. The key-dates block invites full proposals by 25 January 2026 and sets them due 17 February 2026. The open-intake line says 17 February 2027 at 4:00 p.m. MDT. Confirm February before you book a researcher. Do not guess which line is the typo. ([Alberta Innovates](https://albertainnovates.ca/funding/campus-alberta-small-business-engagement-casbe-program/))

## Frequently asked questions

### Is AI training eligible for the Canada-Alberta Productivity Grant?

It can be, if the training is instructor-led, tied to the job, and from a third party whose main work has been training for at least two years. The Alberta page lists example courses that include AI for Business and Generative AI Applications. The ministry decides whether the fee is reasonable. KR8TIV training is not pre-approved. Checked 27 November 2026.

### Do I have to apply before training starts?

Yes for this grant. Training that starts before the status reads Application Received is ineligible. That status arrives when the trainee clicks I confirm in the consent email, not when you press submit. Alberta recommends you submit at least 30 days before the start. If you start after that status and before approval, a decline means the bill is yours. Checked 27 November 2026.

### Can business owners be trainees under the Canada-Alberta Productivity Grant?

Yes, since 8 October 2026, if the owner has an active role. Employed family members can be trainees. Contractors and volunteers cannot, unless the person is an owner. Unemployed family and board members cannot. The employer has to have been operational in Alberta for at least one year. Checked 27 November 2026.

### Will AI replace my staff?

It should not be the plan. Orgvue surveyed 1,163 C-suite and senior leaders at large organizations and published the results on 29 April 2025. Of them, 39 percent had made employees redundant as a result of deploying AI, and 55 percent of that redundancy group said the decisions were wrong. The published gains are output gains for people who stay, especially less experienced staff, with a human still reviewing.

### What is a company brain?

One home for processes, prices, voice and templates, so people and tools read the same facts. KR8TIV builds one for $1,000 CAD. You own it. Checked on the live page 27 November 2026.

### What is the difference between a training grant and PrairiesCan or BDC money?

A training grant pays back part of a course after you pay. PrairiesCan is an interest-free repayable contribution of $250,000 to $5 million, up to 50 percent, over a maximum of three years. BDC LIFT is a loan you repay, from $25,000 to $2 million, for profitable firms with at least $1 million in revenue. Checked 27 November 2026.

### Does rural Alberta use AI at the same rate as the cities?

No. Urban Alberta was 19.3 percent and rural Alberta 14.4 percent in the second quarter of 2026, both quality B. Canada rural was 9.9 percent, quality A.

### How do Edmonton employers start AI training for employees?

Name the tasks, write down what never goes into a tool, and file the grant before the first session. KR8TIV trains in Edmonton and across Canada, online or in person. A free one-hour demo, a micro seminar for your team, is how you see the work before you buy a day.

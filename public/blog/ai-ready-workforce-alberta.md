# AI adoption in Alberta: the winners will have the most AI-capable people

This is a plain-text version of https://kr8tiv.ai/blog/ai-ready-workforce-alberta/. The HTML page is the source of truth.

- Author: Matt Haynes, founder of KR8TIV AI (Edmonton, Alberta)
- Published: November 27, 2026. Updated: November 27, 2026.
- Blog: https://kr8tiv.ai/blog/

Alberta trails B.C. on AI adoption, and grants will pay for most of the training. Same crew, more finished work per person, with a human still signing the work that leaves the building.

## The short answer

- The winners keep the crew and finish more work per person. AI is an upgrade, and a human still signs anything that leaves the building.
- AI adoption Alberta sits at 18.4 percent of businesses, behind British Columbia at 25.1. Saskatchewan is 13.6 percent, Manitoba 13.1, and Canada 19.2.
- The money that pays for training is a reimbursement you file before the course starts. Larger federal programs are repayable, and a seminar by itself does not clear those floors.
- Teach the people you have. One company brain, a phone they already talk into, and a human on the outgoing work.

The businesses that win the next decade are the ones whose people can direct AI. Same crew, more finished work per person. AI is an upgrade, and it still needs a human in charge of the number, the safety call, and the email a customer will read. On AI adoption in Alberta, 18.4 percent of businesses used AI to produce goods or deliver services, behind British Columbia at 25.1 percent.

Headcount will not close that gap. Output per person will, if the people you already pay can point the tools at the work and catch the draft before it leaves the building.

Most Alberta firms have not started. Waiting is a choice. So is moving while the firm down the road is still booking a meeting about whether to have a meeting.

This note is the Western Canada picture, the public money that actually pays for staff training, and a plan for the first 90 days. The grants are real. The forms are picky. You want both facts before you pick a date.

## Why does output per employee matter more than headcount?

Canada's scoreboard is labour productivity: real GDP per hour worked in the business sector. In the second quarter of 2026 it rose 1.0 percent, the largest increase since the second quarter of 2020, while hours worked edged down 0.1 percent. Unit labour costs still rose 0.6 percent, a fifth straight quarterly increase. ([Statistics Canada, The Daily, 3 September 2026](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260903/dq260903c-eng.htm))

When Erik Brynjolfsson, Danielle Li and Lindsey Raymond gave a generative assistant to 5,172 customer-support agents, issues resolved per hour rose 15 percent on average. Less experienced and lower-skilled workers improved speed and quality. The most experienced and highest-skilled workers saw small gains in speed and small declines in quality. Those lines are the abstract in the Quarterly Journal of Economics, May 2025. ([Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/140/2/889/7990658)) The Edmonton telling of the same study is in [what AI can and cannot do](https://kr8tiv.ai/what-ai-can-and-cant-do-for-your-business/).

> The biggest documented gains did not come from removing the newest person. They came from giving that person the habits of the best person, with a human still on the call.

BCG's 2025 AI at Work survey covered more than 10,600 leaders, managers and frontline white-collar employees in 11 countries and regions. More than three-quarters of leaders and managers say they use generative AI several times a week, while regular use among frontline employees has stalled at 51 percent. Regular use is sharply higher for employees who receive at least five hours of training and have access to in-person training and coaching. Only one-third say they have been properly trained. ([BCG, 2025](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/ai-at-work-momentum-builds-but-gaps-remain))

## What about firing people and buying the tool instead?

Orgvue published a survey of 1,163 C-suite and senior leaders on 29 April 2025. The firms had at least 2,000 seats in the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom and Ireland, and at least 500 seats in Australia, Hong Kong, Malaysia and Singapore. Of those leaders, 39 percent had made employees redundant as a result of deploying AI. Of that group, 55 percent said they made the wrong decisions about those redundancies. ([Orgvue](https://www.orgvue.com/news/55-of-businesses-admit-wrong-decisions-in-making-employees-redundant-when-bringing-ai-into-the-workforce/)) These are large firms, not an Alberta crew. The hiring-back pattern is already in [what AI can and cannot do](https://kr8tiv.ai/what-ai-can-and-cant-do-for-your-business/). The point still travels to a crew of eight: the tool does not carry the licence, the stamp or the liability.

## How does the Canada-Alberta Productivity Grant treat AI training?

**Who it is for.** An Alberta employer operational for at least one year.

**What it pays.** On 8 October 2026 the province posted three changes: 75 percent of eligible training costs for every trainee, to the $5,000 cap, with employed family members and business owners eligible too. The funding box on that same page still says per employed trainee and a 25 percent employer share. Follow the 8 October notice and the October 2026 guidelines, not that box. ([alberta.ca](https://www.alberta.ca/canada-alberta-productivity-grant), [October 2026 guidelines](https://open.alberta.ca/dataset/14776419-ee5f-49d3-bfb6-9177d0214ef5/resource/cb980216-d2d1-4ea4-b4c2-df6f71c610a4/download/jeti-canada-alberta-productivity-grant-applicant-guidelines-2026.pdf))

The caps run per fiscal year, 1 April to 31 March, and they still bind if you file again. You pay the whole bill, finish, file the completion form, and then the grant pays the eligible share. The ministry of Jobs, Economy, Trade and Immigration will not confirm eligibility by phone or email. It decides after an application is in the portal.

The ministry gives its own examples, not our prices. On $3,000 of eligible costs the grant pays $2,250 and the employer pays $750. On $12,000 the grant stops at $5,000 and the employer pays $7,000. The ministry has to call the fee reasonable. It can ask for other quotes.

### A $2,000 seminar, split across a crew

KR8TIV's seminar is $2,000 CAD for the group, not per person, checked on the live [AI training](https://kr8tiv.ai/services/ai-training/) page on 27 November 2026. Say eight named trainees, and say the ministry accepts an even split.

- Eligible cost per trainee: $2,000 divided by 8, which is $250.
- Grant at 75 percent: $187.50.
- Employer share: $62.50.
- If all eight are approved: $1,500 back, after the completion form.
- You still write the $2,000 cheque first.

The $5,000 trainee cap does not wake up at $187.50. If the ministry treats the full $2,000 as one person's course, the grant is $1,500 and the employer pays $500. File the invoice you can defend. This is arithmetic, not an approval.


**What it needs, and what it will not pay.**

- Training is instructor-led, tied to the job, and finished within 52 weeks of the date the application is received. Online counts as Alberta. In-person training outside Alberta needs a written reason, including the Alberta courses you considered.
- The provider is a third party whose main work has been training for at least two years, with a qualified instructor. Not the employer, a relative, your own staff, or a vendor whose course is only how to use that vendor's product. A private career college has to be on the registry.
- Out: wages, business software licences, subscriptions, conferences, consulting, coaching, mentorship, facility rental, and a diploma or degree whose point is the credential.
- Trainees live in Alberta and are citizens, permanent residents, or persons protected under the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act. Lloydminster, Saskatchewan is the exception if the employer operates and is located in Alberta and the person works in Alberta.
- In: employees, people who already have a job offer, paid employed family (say why), and owners with an active role. A conflict of interest can sink the file. Out: board members, unemployed family, temporary foreign workers, and contractors or volunteers who are not owners.

**How to apply.** File at [capg.alberta.ca](https://capg.alberta.ca/). Alberta recommends you submit at least 30 days before the start. That is a recommendation. Training that starts before the status reads Application Received is ineligible. The status flips when the trainee clicks "I confirm" in the consent email, not when you click submit. You may pay before approval. You may not start before that status. If you start after the status and before approval, and the file is declined or only partly funded, the bill is yours. First come, first served. Example courses on the public page include AI for Business, Generative AI Applications and Microsoft Copilot.

> A course that starts before the application is received is not a grant. It is a story you tell your bookkeeper.

## What can British Columbia, Saskatchewan and Manitoba employers apply for?

### British Columbia

**Who it is for.** Citizens, permanent residents and protected persons, where the result is a job, a better job, or more security.

**What it pays.** The grant is open for 2026/27. The update note is dated 23 March 2026. Training on or after 1 April 2026 pays 80 percent, to $10,000 a person, cap $300,000 a fiscal year. Notice within 60 days, and you may start before it. A denial leaves the invoice with you. The claim is due within 30 days after training starts.

**What it will not pay.** Temporary foreign workers and international students. Diplomas and degrees, in full or in part, and training past 52 weeks. Six factors sort the queue: first-time applicants, small businesses, regions with the greatest shortages, a B.C. public post-secondary trainer, trades apprenticeship at levels 3 or 4, and shortage industries, including AI and quantum computing. Depending on funding, fewer factors means the file can come back unassessed.

**How to apply.** With a Business BCeID. A third party cannot apply for you, and the participant files their own information form. ([WorkBC](https://www.workbc.ca/employers-industry/funding-programs/bc-employer-training-grant), [what is new](https://www.workbc.ca/employers-industry/funding-programs/bc-employer-training-grant/whats-new))

### Manitoba

**Who it is for.** Employers with fewer than 500 employees, including affiliates, a full year of operations, and a permanent base in Manitoba.

**What it pays.** Building Up Manitoba launched 25 May 2026 with $4 million through the Canada-Manitoba Labour Market Transfer Agreements: half the cost, within the caps in the table, for a job, a better job, or more security. Family members qualify only after more than three months on the payroll before you apply. Training on or after 1 April 2026 can be funded, and the agreement cannot run past the fiscal year you apply in, 1 April to 31 March. The training date decides, not the date you pay.

**What it will not pay.** Travel, diplomas, degrees, and job-unrelated professional development. The page names consulting, coaching, mentorships, subscriptions, conferences and seminars. A seminar counts only when it is tied to the job result. The trainer has to be at arm's length, and training has to be the main business.

**How to apply.** On the program page. ([Program page](https://www.gov.mb.ca/jec/busdev/financial/bump/), [news release](https://news.gov.mb.ca/news/index.html?item=73957))

### Saskatchewan

**Who it is for.** Not a general employer. On 19 March 2025 the province said: "As a result of the federal government's LMTA funding cut, the Canada-Saskatchewan Job Grant will be eliminated."

**What it pays.** On 18 November 2026 Innovation Saskatchewan put $65,000 into two providers. Co.Labs received $40,000 for AI adoption training for small and medium-sized businesses, and had trained eight businesses since a September 2026 launch. ComIT received $25,000 for unemployed and underemployed people: more than 120 registrations, 24 selected, 21 finished, and 11 employed, four of those in tech. On 27 March 2026 the province announced $15.6 million over three years for the Canada-Saskatchewan Workforce Tariff Response, through SaskJobs, up to 1,800 workers in steel, softwood lumber and other directly and indirectly tariff-affected sectors.

**What it will not pay.** A general AI course an employer files. The $65,000 is provider funding. The $15.6 million is a trade response.

**How to apply.** There is no employer form in the pages read for this note. ([19 March 2025](https://www.saskatchewan.ca/government/news-and-media/2025/march/19/delivering-essential-skills-training-despite-federal-government-cuts), [18 November 2026](https://www.saskatchewan.ca/government/news-and-media/2026/november/18/innovation-saskatchewan-invests-in-ai-skills-training-and-business-adoption), [27 March 2026](https://www.saskatchewan.ca/government/news-and-media/2026/march/27/media-advisory))

## What federal money exists, and who is it for?

### PrairiesCan is repayable, and it starts at $250,000

**Who it is for.** An incorporated for-profit firm with a year of operations, at least one full-time employee and a staffed facility in Alberta, Saskatchewan or Manitoba. Firms under 500 full-time employees may be preferred. One approval a calendar year. Projects end 31 March 2031.

**What it pays.** A national $500 million add over five years took the initiative to $700 million. A business gets $250,000 to $5 million, interest-free, up to 50 percent, over at most three years, and pays it back. Not-for-profits that support businesses can be non-repayable up to 90 percent. A not-for-profit on a commercial project is normally repayable. The project adopts AI, or commercializes it above technology readiness level 6, and Canadian AI products may be preferred. Other funding has to be confirmed first, and projects that bring it are preferred. Costs can reach back 12 months before a signed application is received, and not before 16 April 2024. For a business or a commercial project, stacked government money stops at 50 percent of eligible capital costs and 75 percent of other eligible costs. Non-commercial projects, and projects by Indigenous recipients, can stack up to 100 percent.

**What it will not pay.** A $2,000 seminar, which does not reach the floor. Research at technology readiness levels 1 to 5, land, refinancing, lobbying, dues, and ongoing operating costs.

**How to apply.** Phone 1-888-338-9378. The program information page lists priorities that are not the national aims: advanced manufacturing, clean resources, clean technology, digital industries, health and biosciences, value-added processing of natural resources, value-added agriculture, and inclusiveness. The home page, dated 26 October 2026, says 31 December 2030, or until the money is committed. The apply page, dated 16 October 2026, says 31 December 2029. Confirm the date when you file. ([PrairiesCan](https://www.canada.ca/en/prairies-economic-development/services/funding/regional-artificial-intelligence-initiative.html), [program information](https://www.canada.ca/en/prairies-economic-development/services/funding/regional-artificial-intelligence-initiative/information.html), [who can apply](https://www.canada.ca/en/prairies-economic-development/services/funding/regional-artificial-intelligence-initiative/eligibility.html), [apply](https://www.canada.ca/en/prairies-economic-development/services/funding/regional-artificial-intelligence-initiative/application-process.html))

### The B.C. federal window has closed

**Who it is for.** Adoption needs three years of operations in British Columbia and 5 to 500 full-time employees. Commercialization needs one year and 1 to 500.

**What it pays.** A repayable contribution of $200,000 to $5 million, up to 50 percent, when a window is open.

**What it will not pay.** On a page dated 23 November 2026, the status line says applications are not being accepted. The body still says a 2026/27 business intake runs until noon Pacific on 23 November 2026. Today is 27 November, so that noon has passed. Later intakes are described as investments starting after 1 April 2027, for non-profits and smaller firms, with no calendar date.

**How to apply.** Not while that status line stands. ([PacifiCan](https://www.canada.ca/en/pacific-economic-development/services/funding/regional-artificial-intelligence-initiative.html))

### BDC LIFT is a loan

**Who it is for.** A Canadian-based firm with at least $1 million in revenue, a current profit, a solid credit history, an up-to-date roadmap, and a qualified Canadian supplier.

**What it pays.** BDC launched LIFT on 24 April 2026: $500 million, to help over 1,000 small and medium-sized firms. Loan terms, read on 27 November 2026 on the program page: $25,000 to $2 million, capital payments postponed up to 12 months, amortization up to 6 years including that postponement. Training and integration, meaning adoption costs, can be financed. ([News release](https://www.bdc.ca/en/about/mediaroom/news-releases/bdc-launches-lift-getting-canadian-smes-off-the-ai-sidelines))

**What it will not pay.** A grant. You repay the loan. Firms under $1 million in revenue, or without a current profit, do not meet the published test.

**How to apply.** Through BDC. The program page is recorded in the sources file, not linked here.

### IRAP is for firms building the technology

**Who it is for.** Small and medium-sized firms developing or adapting generative AI and deep learning inside their own products: research, development, testing and validation. NRC IRAP's AI Assist page was last modified 22 October 2024.

**What it pays.** No typical award is published.

**What it will not pay.** The page does not describe a reimbursement for a staff course.

**How to apply.** Phone 1-877-994-4727. ([NRC IRAP](https://nrc.canada.ca/en/support-technology-innovation/nrc-irap-support-smes-innovating-artificial-intelligence))

### Amii is a project, not a rebate

**Who it is for.** Prairie firms selected for an Advanced Technology Project. Early work was in energy, agriculture and manufacturing.

**What it pays.** On 27 October 2026 PrairiesCan added $2.5 million, for $5.6 million in federal funding, so Amii could partner with 25 new prairie firms.

**What it will not pay.** A course rebate you file yourself. Limited capacity remained when the page was read on 27 November 2026.

**How to apply.** Through Amii, while capacity remains. ([Amii](https://www.amii.ca/updates-insights/amii-and-prairiescan-expand-successful-ai-adoption-program))

### CASBE: the researcher applies

**Who it is for.** A post-secondary researcher, applying with an Alberta firm that has at least two full-time employees and at least two years of operations in the province. The firm does not apply alone. Projects sit at technology readiness levels 2 to 6 and finish within two years.

**What it pays.** Up to $150,000 a year, to a maximum of $300,000 over two years. Meeting every test does not guarantee funding.

**What it will not pay.** An application the firm sends on its own.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is AI training eligible for the Canada-Alberta Productivity Grant?

It can be, if the training is instructor-led, tied to the job, and from a third party whose main work has been training for at least two years. The Alberta page lists example courses that include AI for Business and Generative AI Applications. The ministry decides whether the fee is reasonable. KR8TIV training is not pre-approved. Checked 27 November 2026.

### Do I have to apply before training starts?

Yes for this grant. Training that starts before the status reads Application Received is ineligible. That status arrives when the trainee clicks I confirm in the consent email, not when you press submit. Alberta recommends you submit at least 30 days before the start. If you start after that status and before approval, a decline means the bill is yours. Checked 27 November 2026.

### Can business owners be trainees under the Canada-Alberta Productivity Grant?

Yes, since 8 October 2026, if the owner has an active role. Employed family members can be trainees. Contractors and volunteers cannot, unless the person is an owner. Unemployed family and board members cannot. The employer has to have been operational in Alberta for at least one year. Checked 27 November 2026.

### Will AI replace my staff?

It should not be the plan. AI is an upgrade, and it still needs a human in charge. Orgvue surveyed 1,163 C-suite and senior leaders at large organizations and published the results on 29 April 2025. Of them, 39 percent had made employees redundant as a result of deploying AI, and 55 percent of that redundancy group said the decisions were wrong. The published gains are output gains for people who stay, especially less experienced staff, with a human still reviewing.

### What is a company brain?

One home for processes, prices, voice and templates, so people and tools read the same facts. KR8TIV builds one for $1,000 CAD. You own it. Checked on the live page 27 November 2026.

### What is the difference between a training grant and PrairiesCan or BDC money?

A training grant pays back part of a course after you pay. PrairiesCan is an interest-free repayable contribution of $250,000 to $5 million, up to 50 percent, over a maximum of three years. BDC LIFT is a loan you repay, from $25,000 to $2 million, for profitable firms with at least $1 million in revenue. Checked 27 November 2026.

### Does rural Alberta use AI at the same rate as the cities?

No. In the second quarter of 2026, urban Alberta was 19.3 percent and rural Alberta was 14.4 percent. Rural Canada was 9.9 percent. Rural Alberta trails the cities and still leads rural Canada.

### How do Edmonton employers start AI training for employees?

Name the tasks, write down what never goes into a tool, and file the grant before the first session. KR8TIV trains in Edmonton and across Canada, online or in person. A free one-hour demo, a micro seminar for your team, is how you see the work before you buy a day.

The problem

The work was never the problem. The paperwork was.

A one-crew operation doesn't fail because the blasting is hard. It fails in the gaps: a receipt photographed at a gas station and never entered, a quote that takes three evenings and goes out a week late, a deposit that lands while the job sits unscheduled, an invoice nobody chased because nobody was watching.

Every one of those is an admin problem with a dollar figure attached. The owner was doing all of it after the crew went home — which meant it competed with sleep and usually lost.

  • Per-job profit and margin, quoted versus actual, on one tab.

What we built

Five systems, wired into what they already had.

Nothing was migrated and nothing was replaced. The workbook and drive Evolve already owned became the database; the AI agents were wired in underneath them.

  • 01

    Receipt pipeline

    A photo of a receipt is read, vendor and total extracted, the image filed into the right drive folder with a dated name, and a row written to the expense ledger — no typing, no shoebox, no April panic.

  • 02

    Quoting engine

    Square footage and blast depth go in; a rate table, access factor, and mobilization come out as a branded PDF with deposit terms. A profitability check runs against media, labour, fuel, and overhead before the number is ever sent.

  • 03

    Dispatch tracking

    Every job moves through one pipeline — awaiting acceptance, booked, confirmed, in progress, complete — with the customer record, quote, and photos attached at each step.

  • 04

    6:30 AM digest

    An agent reads the whole operation before dawn and sends today's jobs, overdue follow-ups, quotes about to expire, and the leads that have gone quiet. It runs whether or not anyone remembers to ask.

  • 05

    Watchdog agents

    Standing rules that catch the expensive mistakes: a deposit received but not scheduled, an invoice unpaid past seven days, a quote unanswered, a finished job never invoiced.

How it went in

Built around a working season, not on top of it.

  1. Week 1 — Audit

    We sat inside the operation and mapped where the hours actually went. The output was a written plan: what an agent could take outright, what needed a human, and what it would cost to build.

  2. Weeks 2–3 — Spine

    One workbook became the single source of truth — dispatch, quotes, customers, expenses, leads — with the drive folder structure that mirrors it. No new app for the crew to learn.

  3. Weeks 3–5 — Agents

    Receipt pipeline, quoting engine, and the scheduled digest went in one at a time, each proven on real jobs before the next was started.

  4. Ongoing — Care

    New automations get added as the season demands them. The system watches the business; we watch the system.

Every few weeks an Edmonton owner tells me they need an app. When we dig in, they usually need one of three different things: a website that books jobs, a tool their crew uses on a phone, or one screen that shows them the whole business.

Those are very different projects with very different price tags. This note is about the second and third kind: **business apps**, the internal tools that run your operation.

## First, decide what kind of app you mean

| You say | You probably need | Rough size |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Customers should book online" | A booking feature on your website | Small |
| "My crew needs to log stuff from the truck" | A field app | Small to medium |
| "I want to see everything in one place" | An owner dashboard on top of your data | Small to medium |
| "We need our own app in the App Store" | A public product app | Large |

Most small businesses in Edmonton land in the middle two rows. That is good news, because those are the cheapest to build well.

## What apps cost in Canada

Canadian studios that publish their budgets list roughly **$13,500 to $21,000 for a prototype**, **$21,000 to $85,000 for a first full product**, and **$60,000 to $250,000** for a public app with accounts, payments and two platforms. We collected those ranges for our sister studio's Field Note on app design costs in Canada, which links every source.

Those numbers are for public apps. An internal business app is a different animal:

- It has a small number of known users (you and your crew).
- It does not need an App Store listing.
- It can sit on tools you already pay for, like Google Sheets and Drive.

That is how a focused internal app can cost a few thousand dollars instead of tens of thousands. Our Custom Business App starts at **$3,000 CAD**. With a Company Brain added it is **$3,750**. A field crew app module on its own is **$750**. No GST charged, and final scope is agreed in writing. If the honest answer is "rebuild the whole back office," that is a Full Business Overhaul, from $10,000 to $20,000 depending on the size of the business.

## Buy or build?

Off-the-shelf software is great when it fits. Buy when:

- A common tool already matches how you work.
- You only need the standard features.
- The monthly cost per seat is less than the time it saves.

Build when:

- You keep bending your business around the software.
- You pay for twelve seats and use three. (We have all been there. No judgment.)
- Someone copies the same data between three apps every day.
- Your trade has a way of pricing or scheduling that no tool understands.

There is a middle path, too. Use off-the-shelf where it works (accounting, payroll), and build the thin layer that connects them and shows you what matters.

## What to build first

The first version should be small enough to ship in weeks. A good first scope looks like this:

1. **One place for the data.** Jobs, customers, quotes and expenses in one workbook or database.
2. **One way in from the field.** A phone screen where the crew logs a receipt, a photo or a lead with a tap.
3. **One screen for the owner.** Today's jobs, money in and out, and what needs a decision.

Everything else (inventory, ordering, a CRM, a pricing engine) can come later, once the first three are proven on real work.

## Exhibit A: Evolve

Evolve is one of the business apps we built, this one for Evolve Eco Blasting, an Edmonton crew with no office staff. It has four parts that share one Google Sheet:

- **A field app.** Phone-first. A crew member signs in with a name and a 4-digit PIN and taps one big button to log a receipt, job photo, lead, quote or the morning Field Level Hazard Assessment.
- **The workbook.** One Google Sheet with tabs for jobs, quotes, customers, expenses and more. You can open it and read it yourself.
- **The brain.** The AI that files receipts, prices quotes against your rates and writes the morning report.
- **The dashboard.** The owner's one screen.

Your app will not be Evolve. It will be yours. But we do not start from a blank page either: the patterns are proven, so a custom business app for your trade is mostly fitting: your job types, your price list, your branding, your crew. That is where the time and cost savings come from, and we pass them down.

## How to keep an app project from going sideways

- **Write down the one job it must do.** If the app does nothing else, what does it need to do?
- **Name the users.** Three crew members and an owner is a different app from 400 customers.
- **Keep your data in your own accounts.** If the vendor disappears, the data should not.
- **Ask who owns the code.** It should be you.
- **Test on real work early.** A week of real receipts teaches more than a month of mockups.

## Edmonton-specific things to think about

- **Weather and gloves.** Field apps here need big buttons, offline-friendly capture and screens you can read in snow glare.
- **Alberta safety rules.** Alberta's OHS Code requires employers to assess a work site for hazards before work begins and involve affected workers. A field app can capture that assessment and the sign-off.
- **Privacy.** Alberta's PIPA expects a reasonable purpose and notice for customer information you collect. Keep it in your own accounts and limit who sees it.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does it cost to build a business app in Edmonton?

Canadian studios that publish budgets list roughly $13,500 to $21,000 for a prototype and $21,000 to $85,000 for a first full product. A focused internal business app built on existing tools can cost far less. KR8TIV AI's Custom Business App starts at $3,000 CAD.

### Should I buy software or build a custom app?

Buy when a common tool fits how you already work and you only need it to do the standard thing. Build when you keep bending your business around the software, paying for seats nobody uses, or copying data between three apps.

### How long does it take to build a business app?

A focused first version on tools you already use can be running in a few weeks. A public app with accounts, payments and two app stores takes months.

### Do I own the app you build?

Yes. The code, the documents and the data stay in your accounts.

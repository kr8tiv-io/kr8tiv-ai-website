"AI agent" is the phrase of the year, and most explanations make it sound either magical or terrifying. It is neither. It is software doing a job you would otherwise give to a very literal new hire.

Here is what one actually does, in plain words, with a real example from Edmonton.

## Chatbot, automation, agent: three different things

**A chatbot** answers questions. You type, it replies. Useful on a website. It does not do anything in your business.

**An automation** follows a fixed rule. "When a form is submitted, add a row to the sheet." Fast and reliable, but it breaks the moment the input looks different from what it expected.

**An agent** sits between the two. It can read messy input (a blurry receipt, a voice note, an email reply), work out what it is, decide which step comes next, use your tools to do it, and report what it did. Then it waits for the next thing.

The simplest way to think about it: a chatbot talks, an automation repeats, an agent handles.

## What an agent needs to be useful

An agent is only as good as three things:

1. **Your facts.** Rates, price list, job types, customers, the way you like things named. Without them it guesses, and guessing is where AI makes things up. This is why we build a Company Brain first: one place that holds what your business knows.
2. **Access to your tools.** A spreadsheet to write to, a folder to file in, an email account to draft from. Only the ones it needs.
3. **Rules about what it may not do.** The most important one: it never sends money or a price to a customer without a person saying yes.

## A normal Tuesday with an agent

This is a typical day for a crew running Evolved, the open-source business system we built for Evolve Eco Blasting in Edmonton. The times are illustrative. The jobs are what the system actually does.

**6:30 AM.** Before anyone is awake enough to ask, the morning report lands: today's jobs, overdue follow-ups, quotes about to expire, deposits that came in but are not scheduled, and the leads that have gone quiet.

**8:45 AM.** A crew member photographs a fuel receipt in the truck and taps one button. A few times a day the agent reads everything the crew logged. It reads the vendor, date and amount, files the image in the right folder, and writes the expense row against the right job.

**11:30 AM.** After a job walk, the crew logs the measurements and photos. The agent prices the job from the owner's own rate table, checks it against material, labour, fuel and overhead so it is not under break-even, and builds a branded quote PDF. Then it stops and waits. The owner reads it and approves it.

**2:15 PM.** A customer replies to a quote by email. The agent turns the reply into a to-do so it does not get buried.

**4:00 PM.** The agent sweeps the money: a finished job with no invoice, an invoice unpaid past seven days. Each one becomes a flag on the owner's dashboard.

**8:00 PM.** Nobody does paperwork.

None of those jobs is clever on its own. The value is that all of them happen, every day, without anyone remembering to do them.

## Where agents go wrong

Agents fail in predictable ways. Plan for them:

- **No facts, so it guesses.** Fix: give it your real price list and documents.
- **Too much access.** Fix: give it only the folders and accounts the job needs.
- **Nobody checks the work.** Fix: every action logged in a sheet you can read, and a person approves anything that leaves the building.
- **It runs in a black box.** Fix: keep the data in your own accounts and own the code.

## Is anyone actually using this?

Yes, and the numbers are climbing. Statistics Canada's second-quarter 2026 survey found **19.2 percent** of Canadian businesses had used AI in the previous 12 months, triple the 6.1 percent from 2024. Among the businesses using AI, **28.2 percent** used virtual agents or chatbots, **24.8 percent** used large language models, and **13.7 percent** used AI-based decision-making systems, up from 5.7 percent a year earlier.

Urban businesses are about twice as likely as rural ones to use AI (21.0 percent against 9.9 percent). For an Edmonton business that is a nudge, not a deadline.

Source: Statistics Canada, catalogue 11-621-M, "Analysis on artificial intelligence use by businesses in Canada, second quarter of 2026," released 11 June 2026.

## How to try one without betting the business

1. Pick one job from your week that repeats and that you would happily never do again.
2. Write the rules a new hire would need to do it.
3. Give an agent only that job, only the tools it needs, and a person to approve the result.
4. Run it for two weeks on real work. Keep it if it saves time. Kill it if it does not.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is an AI agent in simple terms?

Software that can read information, decide which step comes next, use tools like a spreadsheet or email to do it, and report what it did. A chatbot answers questions. An agent does the work.

### What is the difference between an AI agent and automation?

A regular automation follows fixed rules: when this happens, do that. An agent can handle messy input, like a blurry receipt or a rambling voice note, and work out which rule applies.

### Can an AI agent make mistakes?

Yes. That is why a good setup logs everything the agent does, and makes a person approve anything that sends money or a price to a customer.

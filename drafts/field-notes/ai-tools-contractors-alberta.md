Contractors do not have an AI problem. They have an evening problem. The work happens on site. The paperwork happens at the kitchen table at 9 PM.

Most AI marketing aimed at trades talks about chatbots and "transformation." This note skips that and covers the jobs where AI actually saves an Alberta crew time, plus the rules you need to stay inside.

## 1. Receipts that file themselves

Fuel, materials, a rental, lunch for the crew. Every one is a receipt, and every one ends up crumpled in the truck.

**What the tool does:** a crew member snaps a photo. The AI reads vendor, date, amount and GST, files the image in a dated folder, and adds a row to the expense sheet with the job it belongs to.

**The CRA part:** the Canada Revenue Agency accepts electronic images of paper records if the image is an accurate copy and the important details are legible. To throw the paper away, CRA says your imaging should meet the Canadian standard it points to (CAN/CGSB 72.34, Electronic Records as Documentary Evidence). If you cannot meet it, keep the originals. Either way, records must be kept **at least six years from the end of the last tax year they relate to**.

Our honest advice: let the system file the image the moment it is taken, and keep a box for the paper until your accountant says otherwise.

Sources: CRA, "Acceptable format, imaging paper documents and backing up electronic files" and Information Circular IC78-10, both on canada.ca, checked 23 Nov 2026.

## 2. Quotes the same day

A job walk ends with measurements on a notepad. Then the quote takes three evenings because pricing lives in the owner's head.

**What the tool does:** the crew enters the measurements and a few photos. The AI prices it against your own rate table, adds access, mobilization and deposit terms, and builds a branded PDF. Before it goes anywhere, it checks the price against material, labour, fuel and overhead so no job is quoted under break-even.

**The rule:** a person approves every quote before it reaches a customer. The AI does the math. You do the deciding.

## 3. Hazard assessments on a phone

Alberta's Occupational Health and Safety Code (Part 2, section 7) says an employer must assess a work site and identify existing and potential hazards **before work begins**, prepare a written report of the hazards and controls, and record the date. Section 8 says affected workers must be involved.

Paper Field Level Hazard Assessments get done, then get lost, then get rewritten when an inspector asks.

**What the tool does:** a phone-first form the crew fills out before starting, with a sign-off per worker, stored with the job. A "report a hazard" button can email the owner straight away.

This is not legal advice, and a form does not replace a safety program. It makes the paperwork you already owe easier to do and impossible to lose.

Source: Alberta OHS Code, Part 2, sections 7 and 8, via open.alberta.ca, checked 23 Nov 2026.

## 4. A morning report before the first coffee

**What the tool does:** at 6:30 AM a short message lands: today's jobs and addresses, overdue follow-ups, quotes about to expire, deposits received but not scheduled, invoices unpaid past seven days.

It replaces the mental checklist that lives in the owner's head and falls apart during the busy season.

## 5. Getting paid

Most money leaks in the trades are not big. A finished job nobody invoiced. A deposit that landed but the job never got booked. An invoice that sat unpaid because nobody noticed.

**What the tool does:** standing rules watch for those gaps and raise a flag. Nothing is sent automatically. It just makes sure someone notices.

## What not to buy (yet)

- **A chatbot on your website** before your quoting is fixed. Faster leads into a slow quote process just means more people waiting.
- **An all-in-one platform** that needs the whole crew to change how they work mid-season.
- **Anything that holds your data hostage.** Your jobs and customers should live in your own accounts.

## How to pick your first tool

1. Ask the crew which piece of paperwork they hate most. Believe them.
2. Ask yourself which chore eats your evenings. Usually it is quoting or receipts.
3. Start with the one that costs the most hours, and prove it for two weeks before adding anything else.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I throw out paper receipts if I photograph them?

CRA accepts electronic images of paper records if the image is an accurate, legible copy. To destroy the paper, the imaging must meet the Canadian standard CRA points to (CAN/CGSB 72.34). If you are not sure your process meets it, keep the originals. Records must be kept at least six years from the end of the tax year they relate to.

### Does a hazard assessment on a phone count in Alberta?

Alberta's OHS Code requires a written hazard assessment before work begins, with affected workers involved and the date recorded. A phone form can capture that in writing with a sign-off, as long as the content meets the Code. Check with your safety association if you are unsure.

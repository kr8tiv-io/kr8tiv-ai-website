Most Edmonton business owners I talk to do not want an AI strategy. They want their Sunday nights back.

That is a good instinct. The businesses getting real value out of AI right now are not the ones with a vision deck. They are the ones that picked one boring, repeating job and handed it to software that does not get tired.

This is how we decide where to start, what it costs, and what should stay human.

## Where Canadian businesses actually are with AI

You are not late. Statistics Canada's survey for the second quarter of 2026 found that **19.2 percent of Canadian businesses** had used AI to produce goods or deliver services in the previous 12 months. That is triple the 6.1 percent from the second quarter of 2024. In the same survey, **40 percent of businesses said AI was not relevant to them**.

Two things stand out. Use is climbing fast. And a lot of owners still look at AI and see chatbots and robot art, not the paperwork it could take off their plate. The gap between those two groups is where a small Edmonton business can get ahead without spending like a bank.

Source: Statistics Canada, "Analysis on artificial intelligence use by businesses in Canada, second quarter of 2026" (catalogue 11-621-M, released 11 June 2026).

## The test for a good first automation

A task is a good first automation when it passes four checks:

1. **It repeats.** Every day or every week, not twice a year.
2. **It follows the same steps.** If you could write it on an index card, software can do it.
3. **Somebody hates doing it.** That is how you know it is eating time.
4. **A mistake is cheap to catch.** Start where a wrong answer is annoying, not expensive.

If a task fails the fourth check, it can still be automated. It just needs a person to approve the result before it goes anywhere. More on that below.

## Five jobs most Edmonton businesses can hand over first

These come up in almost every audit we do, whether the business blasts driveways, fixes furnaces or runs a two-chair salon.

### Receipts

A receipt photo goes in. Vendor, date, amount and tax come out, the image is filed in the right folder with a sensible name, and a row lands in the expense sheet. Nobody types anything. At tax time the shoebox is already a spreadsheet.

### Quote follow-ups

Quotes go out and then nothing happens, because nobody is watching. An automation can flag every quote with no answer after three or five days and draft the follow-up for you to send.

### The morning report

One short message before the day starts: what is booked, what is overdue, which quotes expire this week, which leads have gone quiet. It sounds small. It is the thing owners tell us they would miss first, right after coffee.

### Lead capture

Web forms, missed calls and Facebook messages all landing in one list, with the date, the source and the next step. No more leads living in three inboxes and one memory.

### Invoice chasing

A finished job with no invoice, or an invoice unpaid after seven days, raises a flag. The money was always there. It just needed someone to notice.

## Keep people in charge of the money

Here is the rule we build everything around: **software can draft, sort, file and remind. A person approves anything that sends money or a price to a customer.**

That one rule takes most of the fear out of AI. If the system misreads a receipt, you see it in the sheet. If it drafts a weird follow-up, you do not send it. Nothing leaves the building without a yes.

It also matters for privacy. Alberta's Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA) expects businesses to collect personal information for a reasonable purpose, tell people why, and protect it. A good setup keeps customer data in your own Google or Microsoft account, limits who can open it, and logs what the automation did.

## How to start this week

You do not need us to take the first step.

1. For one week, write down every task that you or your team repeat. Just the name and roughly how long it took.
2. Circle the three that pass the four checks above.
3. Pick the one that costs the most evenings.

If you want help with step four, the AI Ops Audit is built for exactly that. If you would rather try it yourself, start with receipts. It is the easiest win and the one you will notice at tax time.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best first thing to automate in a small business?

Pick the task that repeats every week, follows the same steps every time and costs someone an evening. For most Edmonton owners that is receipts, quote follow-ups or a daily summary of what is booked, owed and overdue.

### How much does AI automation cost for a small business in Alberta?

At KR8TIV AI a single automation starts from $250 CAD, and the AI Ops Audit that finds the right first one is $495 CAD. Larger systems are quoted in writing. No GST charged.

### Do I need new software to automate my business?

Usually not. We build on the tools most small businesses already have, like Google Sheets, Drive and email, so your team keeps working the way it does now.

### Will AI automation replace my staff?

It replaces the copying, chasing and retyping. The decisions, the customer calls and anything that moves money stay with a person.

### Is it safe to put customer information into an AI system in Alberta?
